Organisms

Read-only surfaces on the desktop, under the windows, in the part of the screen the top and bottom membranes leave free. A cell speaks when something happens; an organism is there because it was put there to be looked at. The specification is ORGANISMS.md; this page is how it looks and moves.

The contract

Three rules change; the rest of the style guide holds as it is.

Changed

  • It speaks at rest. Figures are allowed: the eye goes to an organism on purpose, it does not pass it a hundred times an hour.
  • No thread, no origin. Born in place, growing from its own centre, like the launcher.
  • No input. The pointer passes through to the desktop. Only while arranging can it be dragged.

Kept

  • Colour is state, form is domain — the CPU ring is the CPU ring.
  • Motion is data: no seconds ticking, no idle animation.
  • Two voices, glass with rim, panel radius 20, no shadow — it stands on the wallpaper, not above something.
  • Conditional where it means something: media and weather leave when they have nothing true to say.

On the desktop

A 1920 × 1080 screen with a top and a bottom membrane. On an empty workspace the organisms are the desktop; open a window and they are simply under it. Arrange lifts them above the windows: drag them — they snap to the free area's edges, the screen's centre lines, each other's edges and the 24 px gap.

On a light wallpaper

The glass and the rim carry the surface on a pale picture too; no halo, no glow.

Open

Bare or glassEvery organism here is a glass panel. A bare clock — figures straight on the wallpaper — would read as part of the picture, but on a light or busy wallpaper only a glow would keep it legible, and the style guide forbids glow. Kept as a question rather than as an option.
